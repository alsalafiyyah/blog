### A Brief Biography of Abdul Razzaq ibn Afifi ibn Atiyah

**Name:**
’Abdul-Razzaq ibn ’Afifi ibn ’Atiyyah

**Birth:**
He was born in Shanshure, affiliated with the Ashmoun Center, Menoufia Governorate, in the year 1323 AH.

**Education:**
He completed his primary, secondary, and higher section education. Upon finishing his studies, he was tested and granted the Alimiyyah certificate in 1351 AH. He then studied the specialization stage in the "branch of Fiqh and its foundations" (Usul al-Fiqh), and was awarded the specialization certificate in Fiqh and Usul al-Fiqh after passing the examination. All of these studies took place at Al-Azhar in Cairo.

**Career:**
He was appointed as a teacher in the scientific institutes affiliated with Al-Azhar and taught there for several years. Afterward, he was seconded to the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia to teach within the Saudi educational system in 1368 AH (1949 CE). He was appointed as a teacher at Dar Al-Tawhid in Taif, then transferred two years later to the Onaizah Scientific Institute in Muharram 1370 AH. Later, he was transferred to Riyadh at the end of Shawwal 1375 AH to teach in the scientific institutes affiliated with His Eminence Sheikh Muhammad bin Ibrahim Al Al-Sheikh.

He was subsequently transferred to teach at the Colleges of Sharia and Arabic Language, and was appointed Director of the Higher Institute for Judiciary in 1385 AH. In 1391 AH, he was transferred to the General Presidency of Scholarly Research, Ifta, Da'wah, and Guidance, where he was appointed Deputy Chairman of the Permanent Committee for Scholarly Research and Ifta, while also serving as a member of the Council of Senior Scholars in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

Allah blessed him with exceptional gifts of strong memory, keen observation, and deep legal intuition. He dedicated his efforts to seeking knowledge beyond the halls of Al-Azhar, focusing on the sciences of language, Tafsir, Usul, Islamic creed (Aqidah), Sunnah, and Fiqh. Consequently, whenever he spoke about any of these sciences, the listener would think it was his sole specialization to which he dedicated all his time. He also paid special attention to studying the conditions of various sects and groups.

These qualities drew students of knowledge to him at all times to learn from him, and countless people benefited from his knowledge. He supervised the graduate theses of several students, participated in thesis defense committees, delivered regular lessons for students of knowledge in the mosque whenever possible, gave lectures, and participated in religious awareness programs during the Hajj season.

**His Students:**
It is very difficult to enumerate all the students who studied under him, but these are the most prominent:

1. Muhammad al-Salih al-Uthaymeen
2. Abdullah bin Abdul Rahman al-Ghadyan
3. Salih bin Fawzan al-Fawzan
4. Salih bin Muhammad al-Luhaidan
5. Abdullah bin Abdul Rahman bin Jibreen
6. Abdulaziz bin Abdullah Al Al-Sheikh
7. Abdullah bin Abdul Rahman al-Bassam
8. Abdullah bin Hasan bin Qu'ud
9. Salih bin Abdul Rahman al-Atram
10. Rashid bin Khunain
11. Abdullah bin Sulaiman bin Manea

These are his most prominent students, while the total number is many times this figure.

You will notice, my dear brother, that the venerable Sheikh Abdul Razzaq Afifi is the "Sheikh of Sheikhs" and the teacher of this generation of exceptional scholars whom we have lived alongside. May Allah have mercy on those who have passed away and preserve those who are still living.

## Praise from Scholars

**Sheikh Abdul Aziz bin Baz (may Allah have mercy on him) said:**

> "I know of virtue Sheikh Abdul Razzaq Afifi (may Allah have mercy on him) for his humility, abundant knowledge, good conduct, sound creed, and his tremendous dedication to performing his work in the best possible manner. He is among the finest of scholars in terms of creed, knowledge, da'wah, and teaching. He spent nearly fifty years in this pursuit. May Allah multiply his reward, elevate his rank, grant the Muslims a righteous successor for him, and rectify his offspring. End."

**Sheikh Muhammad al-Salih al-Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him) said:**

> "Sheikh Abdul Razzaq Afifi (may Allah have mercy on him) possessed a sound intellect, foresight, and a tendency toward silence unless speaking was good, alongside the deep-rooted knowledge and excellent teaching ability bestowed upon him by Allah, with very little fluff or verbosity in his speech. His opinion was consistently successful and correct. I ask Almighty Allah to grant him reward and pleasure, and to gather us, him, and our believing brothers in the highest gardens of Paradise; indeed, He is the Bestower, the Gracious. End."

**Sheikh Al-Albani (may Allah have mercy on him) said:**

> "Sheikh Abdul Razzaq Afifi (may Allah have mercy on him) is one of the elite scholars and among the few in whom we see the hallmarks of the people of knowledge: their manners, gentleness, deliberateness, and pious understanding. I met him more than once during the Hajj seasons, and I would listen to his scholarly answers to the inquiries of the pilgrims. They were well-crafted answers that indicated deep jurisprudence and a manifest adherence to the methodology of the Salaf. End."

**Sheikh Abdulaziz Al Al-Sheikh (may Allah have preserve him) said:**

> "The Sheikh was one of the prominent virtuous figures whom Allah enabled to nurture generations... He possessed vast knowledge with extensive insight into Hadith, Tafsir, Fiqh, Usul al-Fiqh, and the Arabic language. Countless cohorts graduated under his supervision. He used to deliver lectures after Isha prayers in the mosque of Sheikh Muhammad bin Ibrahim covering Tafsir, and his lessons were beneficial and his guidance valuable. After coming to the Kingdom, Sheikh Abdul Razzaq worked as a teacher at Dar Al-Tawhid, then moved to the scientific institutes, then served as a member of the Permanent Committee for Scholarly Research, and then as a member of the Council of Senior Scholars... He (may Allah have mercy on him) was an exemplary scholar who acted upon his knowledge. End."

**Sheikh Saleh Al-Sadlan (may Allah forgive him) said:**

> "If I have a word to single out among my teachers, it is Sheikh Abdul Razzaq Afifi. He taught me extensively across multiple educational stages; during my bachelor's degree, he taught me Hadith and Usul al-Din, and he taught me Usul al-Tafsir at the Higher Institute for Judiciary, as well as Fiqh during my final year at the Institute of Judiciary... You could hardly sit with him for a short while without leaving with a scholarly, literary, or moral benefit. I knew him to be someone who disliked speaking ill of anyone. Furthermore, he was distinguished by clarity of expression, and I have never seen a teacher like him in conveying information with so little redundancy. His admirers were many, and we could not have imagined the massive attendance from all groups at his funeral. End."
